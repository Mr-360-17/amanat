# Judges' Q&A: LegacyVault (blockchain + keeper module)

Short answers first, detail after. All numbers are from our own tests.

---

### 1. Why blockchain? Couldn't a normal database do this?
**Short:** a database has an owner who can change the rules or the data. Here nobody can, including us.

The whole point is deciding *fairly* when to release someone's legacy. On-chain, the deadlines, the 2-of-3 rule
and the safety delay are public code that can't be changed or bypassed after deployment. Every check-in and
confirmation is a timestamped public record, so a family, a bank or a court can verify *who* did *what* and *when*.
The state also survives even if our company or servers disappear.

### 2. Why not put everything on-chain?
**Short:** privacy, cost and permanence. We put only what needs *trust* on-chain.

Personal documents and account details must never be public or permanent. So the encrypted vault stays off-chain
(teammates' backend), and the chain stores only: addresses, the state, timestamps, confirmation counts and a
**32-byte hash** of the encrypted vault. The hash proves the vault wasn't swapped or edited, without revealing anything.

### 3. What if the keeper agent goes offline?
**Short:** nothing breaks. The process is only delayed, and anyone can take over.

`startGrace()` and `release()` are **permissionless but condition-gated**: anyone can call them, but the contract
only accepts them when the rules allow (deadline passed; 2 confirmations + delay passed). A contact, a
beneficiary or a second keeper can push the vault forward. The agent has no special power. It can't skip a step
or release early. It also survives RPC outages: it retries with backoff (10 s → 20 s → 40 s → 60 s max) and never
crashes (tested). The UI shows 🔴 OFFLINE when its heartbeat is older than 3 check intervals.

### 4. What if the owner just forgets to check in?
**Short:** several safety nets before anything happens.

1. **Reminder** when 50% or less of the check-in period is left.
2. **Grace period** after the deadline (real: 7 days). One click restores everything.
3. Contacts **can't confirm during grace**. The owner gets the full window.
4. Even after 2 confirmations there's a **release delay** (real: 2 days) where the owner can still check in or cancel.
Any check-in starts a **new round**, so old confirmations are wiped.

### 5. What if trusted contacts are malicious?
**Short:** they need 2 of 3, they must wait out the grace period, and the owner can still veto during the delay.

- One contact alone can do nothing.
- Nobody can confirm before the grace deadline, and each contact confirms at most once per round.
- After 2 confirmations the owner still has the release delay to cancel.
- Contacts can't be changed during GRACE/CONFIRMED, so an attacker can't swap in their own addresses mid-process.

**Residual risk (honest):** if 2 contacts collude *and* the owner is unreachable for the grace period plus the delay,
they can trigger release. Longer real-world periods, email/SMS alerts to the owner, and careful choice of
contacts reduce this.

### 6. Why 2-of-3?
**Short:** tolerates one bad actor *and* one unreachable person.

1-of-3 lets a single person act alone. 3-of-3 fails if any one contact is unreachable or has also died. 2-of-3 is
the classic balance between safety and availability. The threshold is a constant in the contract, easy to change.

### 7. Can it move money?
**No.** The contract has no `payable` functions, holds no funds, makes no transfers and no external calls.
"Release" is a verifiable **signal** that the conditions were met. What gets released is access to the
encrypted vault, handled off-chain. This keeps the attack surface tiny.

### 8. How would a bank or institution integrate?
**Short:** by reading public, verifiable state. No data sharing needed.

A bank (or lawyer, or insurer) could check the contract: is it RELEASED, when, confirmed by which addresses,
and does the document hash match what they were given? The `VaultReleased` / `DeathConfirmed` events are a tamper-proof
audit trail. In production this would sit next to (not replace) legal processes like a death certificate.

### 9. How are keys secured?
**Prototype:** throwaway testnet keys, generated by a script, stored only in `.env` (gitignored, never printed or
committed). The backend's `POST /vault/hash` needs a shared API key, compared in constant time.

**Production:**
- The owner and contacts use their own wallets (ideally hardware wallets). We never hold their keys.
- The keeper key would live in a KMS/HSM. It can only do safe things: permissionless steps plus storing the hash.
- Honest caveat: a stolen keeper key could overwrite the vault hash, but could **not** release anything early.
  Production fix: owner-signed hashes or an append-only hash history.

### 10. What if the RPC goes down?
Nothing on-chain is lost. The state lives on the chain, not in our servers. Deadlines use **block time**, so an
outage only delays the automatic steps. The keeper retries with backoff. The UI keeps showing the last known data
with a warning banner. Production: multiple RPC providers with failover.

### 11. How does it scale?
- **Cost:** a full cycle (check-in, grace, 2 confirmations, release) is about 0.3M gas, which is **under 0.001 tMSTC** at 1 gwei.
  Deployment is about 1.57M gas.
- **Now:** one contract per owner (simple, isolated).
- **Production:** a factory or a single multi-vault contract (vaults in a mapping), an event indexer instead of
  per-page log scans, and a keeper that watches many vaults from indexed events. It only acts when a deadline passes.

### 12. What's innovative here?
- **An autonomous agent that enforces a legal-style timeline**, while the contract guarantees the agent can't cheat.
- **A human safety net at every step:** grace, 2-of-3, a mandatory delay, and a one-click owner veto that works up to the last moment.
- **Round-based confirmations**, so stale or old votes can never be replayed.
- **Privacy by design:** a verifiable process with zero personal data on-chain.

### 13. What would you change for production?
- Security audit. Realistic periods (e.g. 30 d / 7 d / 2 d; `DEMO_MODE=false` already supports this).
- Email/SMS/push notifications to owner and contacts, not just in-app.
- Redundant keepers + multiple RPCs. Keeper key in a KMS.
- Gasless confirmations for non-crypto contacts (account abstraction / meta-transactions).
- Owner-signed vault hashes or a hash history.
- Contact rotation with a timelock. Configurable thresholds.
- Factory / multi-vault contract + event indexer. Legal review.
